An IEP year has a dozen dates. They should not live in your memory.

An annual review every twelve months. A reevaluation every three years. An evaluation timeline that runs on your state's clock, in school days or calendar days depending on where you live. Transition planning that must start at an age your state chooses. Each date arrives on paper, in a different letter, months apart.

This site does two things. It gives you a free planner that works out this year's deadlines from the dates you already have. And it is preparing a simple organiser that keeps those deadlines, reminds you before each one, and holds your record of the year in one place.

The free IEP Year Planner

Enter the dates on your child's current paperwork — last annual review, last evaluation, the date you signed consent — and see the deadlines for this year, worked out from the federal rules and your state's own timeline.

It runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere, and there is no account.

Every state runs its own clock

Federal law gives schools 60 days to complete an initial evaluation after you consent — unless your state has set its own timeline, and most have: 25 school days in one state, 90 calendar days in another, a clock that starts at referral rather than consent in a few. The pages below list each state's figures exactly as published in its official rules, with the source linked on every page.

What this site is not

It is not advice. It cannot tell you what your child should receive, whether a decision was right, or what to say in a meeting. For that kind of help, trained people exist and they are free: every state has a federally funded Parent Training and Information Center. What this site does is narrower and, we think, still missing: it keeps the dates and the paper in order.
