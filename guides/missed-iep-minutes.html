Missed IEP service minutes: what to do when the school delivers less than the IEP promises

The minutes in an IEP are not a target — they are a commitment. When sessions are shortened, skipped or quietly dropped, your child is owed the education those minutes were meant to buy. Here is how to spot it, document it, and get it made up.

Step 1

Know exactly what was promised

Find the services page of the IEP. It states each service, how many minutes, how often (per week or per month), where, and whether it is one-to-one or in a group. That line — not the school's schedule, not the therapist's caseload — is the promise everything else is measured against.

Step 2

Keep your own log, every week

For each service, write down the date, what was scheduled, and what actually happened — full session, shortened, or missed. Two minutes a week. If a dispute ever comes, the parent with the dated log is the one who gets believed. (Our free service log does exactly this, and totals promised versus delivered for you.)

Step 3

Ask for the school's records

You can ask, in writing, for the school's own service delivery records for any period. Schools keep schedules and session logs; a polite written request usually produces them. Compare them with your log — where they disagree, ask why, in writing.

Step 4

Ask for compensatory education

The remedy for missed services is usually compensatory education: extra sessions or services that make up for what your child lost. Ask in writing, attach your log, and name the number of minutes you believe are owed. Schools agree to make-up services far more often when the request is written and counted.

Step 5

If the school will not fix it

  1. 1

    Write to the school and the district's special education office. State the promised minutes, the delivered minutes, and what you are asking for. Keep every reply.

  2. 2

    Get free help. Your Parent Center can review your log and tell you what usually works in your state.

  3. 3

    File a state complaint. Within one year of each missed session (34 CFR 300.153). The state generally must investigate and decide within 60 days (34 CFR 300.152) — and it can order the school to make the minutes up.

  4. 4

    Ask about mediation. Free mediation (34 CFR 300.506) often settles a minutes dispute faster than a formal case.

Questions parents ask

Missed IEP minutes — FAQ

How do I know if IEP service minutes were missed?

Compare the IEP itself — the promised minutes, frequency and setting — with what actually happened: the school's service records, the therapist's schedule, and your child's account. You can ask the school, in writing, for the service delivery records for any period.

Does the school have to make up missed IEP minutes?

There is no automatic federal formula, but a school that fails to deliver the services in an IEP has failed to provide the education the IEP promises. The usual remedy is compensatory education — extra services to make up for what was missed. Ask for it in writing, with your log attached.

What counts as evidence of missed minutes?

Your own dated log is powerful: date, what was scheduled, what actually happened. So are the school's own records — schedules, attendance, service logs — which you can request. Contemporaneous notes beat memories, every time.

How long do I have to complain about missed services?

A state complaint must be filed within one year of the missed services (34 CFR 300.153), and the state generally must decide within 60 days (34 CFR 300.152). Do not wait — the clock runs from each missed session, not from when you noticed.
