IEP timelines in Rhode Island

The dates that govern your child's IEP in Rhode Island, taken from the state's published rules. The official source for each figure is linked at the bottom of this page.

Checking note: Rhode Island's current statute sets this timeframe at 63 calendar days, while the state's regulation text still shows 60. We show the statute's figure (63); your Parent Center can confirm which applies.
Initial evaluation63 calendar days
The clock starts on the date the parent signs consent for the evaluation.
Annual IEP reviewAt least once every 12 months
Federal rule — it applies in every state, including this one.
ReevaluationAt least once every 3 years, unless you and the school agree in writing that it is not needed.
Federal rule — it applies in every state, including this one.
Transition planningFrom age 14 — it must be in the IEP in effect on that birthday.
RI Department of Education IEP guidance: the student must be invited, and postsecondary goals and transition services considered, beginning when the student is 14 years of age (or younger if appropriate); for students 14 or older the IEP team must consider transition services and invite participating agencies. Consistent with 200-RICR-20-30-6.7.4(A)(2), which applies the federal transition-content rule beginning at age fourteen (14).

CONTRADICTION BETWEEN OFFICIAL SOURCES: R.I. Gen. Laws 16-24-1.1(g) (codified statute page headed 'Effective July 1, 2026,' which has passed) sets the timeframe for conducting initial evaluations and convening a meeting, as described in 200-RICR-20-30-6.7.2(A)(1)(b), at sixty-three (63) calendar days. The regulation text itself (200-RICR-20-30-6, active, refiled effective 01/04/2022 per the RI Department of State) still states sixty (60) calendar days from receipt of parental consent. Recorded 63 as the later, controlling statutory value; the regulation has apparently not yet been conformed.
