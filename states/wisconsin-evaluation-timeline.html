The rule, in the state's own words

What Wisconsin's published rule says

“LEAs are required by law to perform the evaluation within 60 days; these are 60 calendar days, not 60 school days”

The 60 days run from receipt of consent (or of the notice that no additional assessments are needed) under Wis. Stat. §115.78(3), and the timeline runs across holiday breaks. Delay exceptions: the student transfers; the child is unavailable because the parent repeatedly fails or refuses to make the student available; initial SLD evaluations may be extended by mutual written agreement.

Source: Timely Special Ed Evaluation (Indicator 11) — Wisconsin Department of Public Instruction · Last verified 14 November 2026

If the date passes

What happens if the school misses the evaluation deadline

  1. 1

    Write to the school. Ask for the reason and the new date, in writing — and keep their reply. A paper trail is evidence.

  2. 2

    Get free help. Your Parent Center can check the dates with you and tell you what parents in Wisconsin usually do next.

  3. 3

    File a state complaint. It must be filed within one year of the missed deadline (34 CFR 300.153). The state generally must investigate and decide within 60 days (34 CFR 300.152).

  4. 4

    Ask about mediation. Free mediation (34 CFR 300.506) is often faster than a formal dispute — and asking for it does not take away any other right.

Questions parents ask

Evaluation timeline in Wisconsin — FAQ

How long does a school in Wisconsin have to complete an IEP evaluation?

Schools in Wisconsin have 60 calendar days to complete an initial IEP evaluation. The clock starts on the date you sign consent for the evaluation. Federal law (34 CFR 300.301) sets a default of 60 calendar days, but Wisconsin's own timeframe is the one that applies.

When does the evaluation clock start in Wisconsin?

Day one is the date the school receives your signed, written consent for the evaluation. Ask the school to confirm that date in writing — it starts the clock.

How are the days counted in Wisconsin?

Calendar days are every day on the calendar — weekends and holidays count.

What can I do if the school misses the evaluation deadline in Wisconsin?

Put your concern in writing to the school and ask for the reason and the new date — and keep their reply. Your state's Parent Center (free, federally funded) can review the dates with you. You can also file a state complaint: it must be filed within one year of the missed deadline (34 CFR 300.153), and the state generally must decide within 60 days (34 CFR 300.152).
