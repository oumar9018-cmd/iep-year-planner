IEP timelines in Nebraska

The dates that govern your child's IEP in Nebraska, taken from the state's published rules. The official source for each figure is linked at the bottom of this page.

Initial evaluation45 school days
The clock starts on the date the parent signs consent for the evaluation.
Annual IEP reviewAt least once every 12 months
Federal rule — it applies in every state, including this one.
ReevaluationAt least once every 3 years, unless you and the school agree in writing that it is not needed.
Federal rule — it applies in every state, including this one.
Transition planningFrom age 14 — it must be in the IEP in effect on that birthday.
92 NAC 51-007.07A9: transition content is required 'beginning not later than the first IEP to be in effect when the child turns 14,' with measurable postsecondary goals and transition services; NDE's 2026 Model IEP Guidance summarizes Rule 51 as establishing 'transition planning beginning at age 14.'

This state sets two limits — 45 school days or 60 calendar days — and the earlier date is the deadline.

92 Neb. Admin. Code 51-009.04A1 sets a 45-school-day timeline for an initial evaluation, BUT NDE guidance stresses it cannot supersede the federal 60-calendar-day outer limit: the evaluation must be finished within 60 calendar days regardless of school breaks (summer vacation, holidays). Per OSEP clarification cited by NDE, there is no exception suspending the timeline for school breaks.
