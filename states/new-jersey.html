IEP timelines in New Jersey

The dates that govern your child's IEP in New Jersey, taken from the state's published rules. The official source for each figure is linked at the bottom of this page.

Initial evaluation90 calendar days
The clock starts on the date the parent signs consent for the evaluation.
Annual IEP reviewAt least once every 12 months
Federal rule — it applies in every state, including this one.
ReevaluationAt least once every 3 years, unless you and the school agree in writing that it is not needed.
Federal rule — it applies in every state, including this one.
Transition planningFrom age 14 — it must be in the IEP in effect on that birthday.
N.J.A.C. 6A:14-3.7(e)11 (per NJDOE's official Transition Requirements summary): beginning with the IEP in place for the school year when the student will turn 14 (or younger if the IEP team determines appropriate), the IEP must include the student's strengths, interests and preferences and a course of study; (e)12 adds measurable postsecondary goals and full transition services beginning with the IEP for the school year when the student turns 16.

N.J.A.C. 6A:14-3.4(e): the single 90-calendar-day period runs from the Parental Consent to Evaluate Date and covers the whole initial process - evaluation, eligibility determination and, if eligible, development and implementation of the IEP. NJDOE's Indicator 11 reporting requires a documented delay reason whenever any step exceeds 90 days; the code provides an exception where the parent repeatedly fails or refuses to produce the child.
