How to file a special education state complaint in New Jersey

A state complaint is a written statement to the state that your school violated special education law. It is free, it needs no lawyer, and the state must investigate. Two clocks matter: you must file within one year of the violation, and the state generally must decide within 60 days.

Where to file in New Jersey

New Jersey Department of Education, Office of Special Education

Grounds

What a state complaint can cover

  • A missed deadline — the evaluation ran past New Jersey's 90 calendar days clock, or an annual review or reevaluation is overdue. See New Jersey's evaluation timeline.
  • Missed service minutes — the IEP promised sessions that were shortened, skipped or never delivered. How to document missed minutes.
  • An IEP not followed — accommodations, placement or services in the written IEP are not happening in practice.
  • A refused evaluation or service — the school said no and did not give you a prior written notice explaining why.
Before you send it

What to include in the complaint

  • Your name and contact details, signed — a state complaint must be a signed, written statement.

  • Your child's name and school (and address, or contact details if you are homeless).

  • The violation, in one or two sentences — which requirement of special education law the school did not follow.

  • The facts and dates — what was promised, what happened, and when. Attach your deadline calculations and any minutes log.

  • What you want fixed — a proposed resolution: the evaluation completed, the minutes made up, the IEP followed.

  • A copy to the school district — at the same time as you file with the state, send a copy of the complaint to the district. The rules require it, and a complaint that skips this step can stall.

After you file

What happens next

  1. 1

    The state checks your complaint is complete and about special education law.

  2. 2

    An investigator gathers facts — documents and answers from you and from the school.

  3. 3

    You get a written decision, generally within 60 days, addressing each allegation (34 CFR 300.152).

  4. 4

    If the school violated the law, the decision orders corrective action — which can include compensatory education for your child.

Questions parents ask

State complaints in New Jersey — FAQ

How long do I have to file a state complaint in New Jersey?

One year from the date of the violation (34 CFR 300.153). The clock runs from each missed deadline or missed session — not from when you noticed — so file as soon as you can.

Does filing a state complaint in New Jersey cost anything?

No. A state complaint is free, and you do not need a lawyer. The complaint goes to New Jersey Department of Education, Office of Special Education, which must investigate and generally issue a written decision within 60 days (34 CFR 300.152).

What can the state order if my complaint is upheld?

Corrective action aimed at your child and at the violation: compensatory education (make-up services), a reconvened IEP meeting, staff training, or changes to district procedures. The decision is written and must address each allegation you raised.
