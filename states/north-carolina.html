IEP timelines in North Carolina

The dates that govern your child's IEP in North Carolina, taken from the state's published rules. The official source for each figure is linked at the bottom of this page.

Initial evaluation90 calendar days
In this state the clock starts on the date the school receives the referral — not on the date of consent.
Annual IEP reviewAt least once every 12 months
Federal rule — it applies in every state, including this one.
ReevaluationAt least once every 3 years, unless you and the school agree in writing that it is not needed.
Federal rule — it applies in every state, including this one.
Transition planningFrom age 14 — it must be in the IEP in effect on that birthday.
NC DPI's IDEA Part B state application (dpi.nc.gov) states verbatim, citing NC 1503-4.3(b)(2): 'North Carolina requires transition planning beginning at age 14.' NC policy NC 1503-4.1 places initial transition components (needs/preferences/interests, course of study) in the first IEP in effect when the child turns 14, with full measurable postsecondary goals by 16.

REFERRAL-BASED CLOCK, not consent-based: NC Policy NC 1503-2.2(c)(1) requires evaluations conducted, eligibility determined, and for an eligible child the IEP developed and placement completed within 90 days of receipt of a written referral. NC DPI's State Performance Plan page describes Indicator 11 the same way (referral received to placement determined within 90 calendar days). This single clock covers evaluation + eligibility + IEP + placement.
