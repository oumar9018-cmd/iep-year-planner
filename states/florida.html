IEP timelines in Florida

The dates that govern your child's IEP in Florida, taken from the state's published rules. The official source for each figure is linked at the bottom of this page.

Initial evaluation60 calendar days
The clock starts on the date the parent signs consent for the evaluation.
Annual IEP reviewAt least once every 12 months
Confirmed in this state's own published rules.
ReevaluationAt least once every 3 years, unless you and the school agree in writing that it is not needed.
Confirmed in this state's own published rules.
Transition planningFrom age 14 — it must be in the IEP in effect on that birthday.
FS 1003.5716: the planning PROCESS begins during 7th grade or at age 12, whichever occurs first, to identify the need for transition services before the student enters high school or attains age 14, whichever occurs first; subsec. (2) requires the IEP to include the transition statements 'beginning not later than the first IEP to be in effect when the student enters high school, attains the age of 14... whichever occurs first'. Recorded age 14 is the age by which transition content must be in the IEP; the process starts at 12.

FAC Rule 6A-6.0331(3)(g) (applies to consents after June 30, 2015): days NOT counted toward the 60 are (1) all school holidays and Thanksgiving, winter and spring breaks; (2) the summer vacation period; (3) student absences beyond 8 school days in the period. Rule 6A-6.0331(3)(h): parent and district may by mutual written agreement (secured before day 45) extend by up to 30 calendar days.
