State timelines · New Hampshire

IEP timeline in New Hampshire

The short answer

How many days does a school in New Hampshire have to finish an initial IEP evaluation?

60 calendar days

The clock starts on the date you sign consent for the evaluation.

Annual IEP review
12 months

At least once every 12 months — New Hampshire publishes the same rule in its own guidance.

Reevaluation
3 years

At least once every 3 years, unless you and the school agree in writing that one is not needed. (Federal rule, 34 CFR 300.303.)

Transition planning
Age 14

Ed 1109.01(a)(10): a plan for each student with a disability beginning at age 14 (or younger if the IEP team determines appropriate) must include a statement of transition service needs focusing on the student's courses of study. Note the split: the age-14 element is the transition-needs/courses-of-study statement; the full statement of needed transition services follows 34 CFR 300.320(b) at the federal age of 16.

The details

New Hampshire's evaluation clock, in the state's own words

Ed 1107.01(c): for initial evaluations, the evaluation process shall be completed within 60 days after receipt of parental consent. 'Day' is defined in Ed 1102.02(a) by reference to 34 CFR 300.11(a), i.e., calendar day; NHED's Procedural Safeguards Handbook states the rule expressly as '60 calendar days.' (For reevaluations the same 60-day period applies and may be extended by up to 30 days with written consent of the parties, Ed 1107.01(d)-(e).)
